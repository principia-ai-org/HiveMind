# Project LANA and Human-Guided Lean Workflows

## Summary

On 2026-07-17, ZEN University's ZMC published a [Project LANA interim report](https://zen.ac.jp/news/zmcpostevent0717e) on computer-assisted verification of IUT theory and said the project is still suspending judgment on whether IUT proves the abc conjecture. A separate 2026-07-16 [MathCoPilot preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14582) describes a human-in-the-loop Lean workflow for mathematical research and reports that current models still face substantial difficulty on deep domain-specific theorems.

## Why It Matters

Together, the [Project LANA release](https://zen.ac.jp/news/zmcpostevent0717e) and [MathCoPilot preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14582) sharpen the active theorem-proving question: current progress is not just about model pass rates on curated benchmarks, but about whether hard mathematics can be decomposed into formalizable, inspectable workflows without losing target fidelity. The evidence is durable because it concerns advanced-mathematics formalisation practice and model limits on hard theorem-proving tasks.

## Evidence

- [ZEN University release](https://zen.ac.jp/news/zmcpostevent0717e) (official primary source, published 2026-07-17) says Project LANA released an interim report on IUT theory, continues to suspend judgment on the abc-conjecture claim, and aims to prepare remaining points for Lean formalisation. Confidence: high for the release and stated project position.
- [Project LANA report artifact](https://github.com/katobungen/LANA_report_202607/blob/pdf/LANA_report_202607.pdf) (primary artifact linked by the official release) provides the public report file. Confidence: high for artifact availability.
- [MathCoPilot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14582) (primary preprint, submitted 2026-07-16) describes a living proof blueprint, Lean-integrated iterative verification, and topic-driven paper retrieval/formalisation, while reporting that current models struggle on domain-specific PDE theorems. Confidence: medium.

## Uncertainties

- Project LANA's source explicitly does not settle whether IUT proves the abc conjecture.
- MathCoPilot's model-comparison results are preprint-level and need independent replication, code availability, and clearer benchmark details before they should be treated as durable capability measurements.
- Neither source alone proves that AI theorem-proving systems are improving on genuinely hard benchmarks; they mainly clarify workflow design and hard-domain limits.
