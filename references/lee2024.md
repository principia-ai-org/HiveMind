# Why Do Animals Need Shaping? A Theory of Task Composition and Curriculum Learning

*Authors:* Jin Hwa Lee, Stefano Sarao Mannelli, Andrew Saxe
*Link:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18361

## Summary

Animal training and curriculum learning commonly rely on "shaping" — teaching an agent
simpler component tasks before the full, complex target task — and this is used widely
in neuroscience research, but why shaping works and how to design an effective shaping
curriculum lacked a theoretical account.

The authors build an analytical model of deep policy-gradient learning on
compositional reinforcement-learning tasks, using statistical-physics tools to derive
exact learning dynamics. They compare training strategies, focusing especially on
"primitives pretraining," where an agent first learns individual task components
before being trained on the composite task built from them.

Their analysis characterizes how task complexity interacts with the effectiveness of
shaping, and shows how structured curricula that expose useful task primitives first
can produce faster, more robust learning than training directly on the composite task.
For the citing problem, this paper is cited alongside the RL Perceptron as a candidate
toy-model approach — exact, statistical-physics-derived learning dynamics for
policy-gradient training — that could be extended to model how curricula or
compositional training data shape which persona a model develops during fine-tuning.
