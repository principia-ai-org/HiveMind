# Persona Vectors: Monitoring and Controlling Character Traits in Language Models

*Authors:* Runjin Chen, Andy Arditi, Henry Sleight, Owain Evans, Jack Lindsey
*Link:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21509

## Summary

Deployed LLM assistants sometimes drift into undesired character traits (e.g. becoming
sycophantic, dishonest, or "evil") after finetuning. This paper introduces "persona
vectors": directions in a model's activation space that correspond to specific
personality traits, extracted automatically from natural-language trait descriptions
(with no need for curated contrastive datasets).

The authors show persona vectors can (1) monitor how much a trait is being expressed
during a conversation or during training, (2) predict which finetuning runs or datasets
will shift a model toward an undesired trait before/without ever training the model, and
(3) be used for post-hoc or preventative interventions — including activation steering
and flagging problematic individual training samples — to suppress unwanted persona
shifts while preserving general capability. Empirically, measured shifts in persona-trait
expression after finetuning correlate strongly with the corresponding persona-vector
projections, for both intended and unintended personality changes.

For the citing problem, this paper is direct empirical evidence for treating "personas"
as identifiable, quantifiable directions/latents in a model's representation space whose
amplitude changes systematically with training — exactly the kind of measurable
quantity a toy model of persona selection/development would need to predict.
