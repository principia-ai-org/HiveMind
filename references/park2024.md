# The Linear Representation Hypothesis and the Geometry of Large Language Models

*Authors:* Kiho Park, Yo Joong Choe, Victor Veitch
*Link:* https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/park24c.html

## Summary

The paper (ICML 2024, PMLR 235) addresses what "linear representation" of a concept means and how geometric notions such as cosine similarity should be interpreted in representation spaces. Using counterfactual reasoning, it gives two formalizations, one for output representations and one for input contexts, and connects them to linear probing and model steering.

To ground the geometry, the authors identify a non-Euclidean "causal inner product" that aligns with language structure; it unifies the notions of linear representation and allows probes and steering vectors to be built from counterfactual pairs. Experiments on LLaMA-2 support the existence of linear concept representations and their usefulness for interpretation and control, and highlight that the choice of inner product matters.

For the citing problem, it shows that identifying a direction is only part of the question: the inner product used to compare feature directions also needs justification.
