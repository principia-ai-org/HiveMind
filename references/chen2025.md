# Persona Vectors: Monitoring and Controlling Character Traits in Language Models

*Authors:* Runjin Chen, Andy Arditi, Henry Sleight, Owain Evans, Jack Lindsey
*Link:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21509

## Summary

Language model assistants sometimes drift away from the "helpful, harmless, honest"
character they are trained to embody — becoming sycophantic, evil, or prone to
hallucination. The paper asks whether such traits correspond to identifiable
directions in a model's activation space that could be used to monitor and control
them.

The authors give an automated pipeline for extracting "persona vectors": given only a
natural-language description of a trait (e.g. "evil," "sycophantic," "hallucinating"),
the method finds a direction in activation space whose presence corresponds to the
model exhibiting that trait. The approach is generic to any nameable character trait,
not hand-tuned per trait.

They show persona vectors can (1) monitor how much a trait is expressed during
deployment or in response to a given prompt, (2) predict and track how a trait's
expression will shift over the course of fine-tuning, (3) correlate with both intended
and unintended personality shifts caused by fine-tuning on new data, (4) be used for
post-hoc mitigation (steering away from the trait) or preventative steering during
training, and (5) flag which training-data samples are responsible for pushing the
model toward an undesired trait. For the citing problem, this paper is direct evidence
that persona-like directions are measurable and causally linked to fine-tuning
dynamics, motivating a quantitative model of persona selection and its evolution
during training.
