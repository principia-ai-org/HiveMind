# An Explanation of In-context Learning as Implicit Bayesian Inference

*Authors:* Sang Michael Xie, Aditi Raghunathan, Percy Liang, Tengyu Ma
*Link:* https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02080

## Summary

The paper asks why large language models such as GPT-3 can perform in-context
learning — solving a downstream task purely by conditioning on a prompt of
input-output examples, with no gradient updates. It gives a theoretical account:
if pretraining documents exhibit long-range coherence, they can be modeled as
generated by a mixture of latent concepts (formalized as a mixture of Hidden
Markov Models). A pretrained LM implicitly learns this mixture, and at test
time a prompt — even one whose distribution differs from pretraining text —
lets the model infer ("Bayesian infer") which latent concept it should follow,
then generate accordingly. This explains in-context learning as implicit
Bayesian inference over latent concepts rather than as learning a new task
from scratch.

The authors back the theory with a synthetic dataset (GINC) generated from an
explicit mixture of HMMs, on which both Transformers and LSTMs exhibit
in-context learning, and reproduce several real phenomena observed in large
LMs: accuracy improving with model scale and with more in-context examples,
sensitivity to the order of examples, and settings where zero-shot prompting
can outperform few-shot prompting.

Relevant to persona selection because it offers a mechanistic lens — latent
concepts learned during pretraining that a prompt selects among — for how
in-context information could pick out a particular "persona," which the
citing problem wants to model quantitatively and dynamically for RLHF-tuned
LMs.
